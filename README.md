## Step by step on the web or desktop app

The web flow is the reference version, since it labels each step explicitly. Have your phone within reach before you begin.

1. Open the OKX site in a browser, or install the desktop app for Windows or macOS. The web version needs no installation.
2. On the homepage, select **Sign up**.
3. In **Country/region**, pick where you actually live. Read the terms and acknowledge them, then select **Next**. In the app and on the current web page the single checkbox covers the Terms of Service, the Risk and Compliance Disclosure and the Privacy Notice.
4. Enter your email in **Email address**. This is also where the **Referral code** field appears, and it's optional. Select **Sign up**.
5. A six-digit code lands in your inbox. Enter it in **Enter your email code** within 10 minutes, then select **Next**.
6. Enter your mobile number in **Phone number** and select **Verify now**.
7. A second six-digit code arrives by SMS. Enter it in **Enter your phone code** within 10 minutes, then select **Next**.
8. Set your password in the **Password** field: upper and lower case letters, numbers and symbols. OKX's own guidance is blunt about this one. Never share it with anyone, including people claiming to work for the company.
9. Verify your identity. This is the step that unlocks the account, and it's covered in detail further down.

OKX describes the whole thing as a few minutes of work, which matches the step count. The 10-minute windows on each code are the only real time pressure.

## Country/region means where you live, not where your passport is from

Your nationality doesn't disqualify you by itself. OKX's own example: someone who is a citizen of Country A but lives in Country B is assessed on Country B, its local rules, and whether the entity operating there supports the service. Select the country or region where you currently and ordinarily reside, not your passport-issued country, not a permanent residence permit held elsewhere, and not a temporary travel destination.

If your country doesn't appear in the list, the current registration process doesn't support that location. OKX explicitly asks users not to pick a country that doesn't match their real residence to get around a missing option, and the reason is practical rather than moral: the account would sit under the wrong service entity.

Being able to register also doesn't mean every product is open to you. Trading pairs, payment methods, deposit and withdrawal options and derivatives services can all vary by your account's service entity, your country of residence, your account type, and what checks you've completed. What you see inside the account is the final answer.

## When sign-up stalls: what to check

Most failures come down to one of three things, and the fixes are quick.

**The code or the contact details.** Re-check the email address and phone number character by character, and confirm the code hasn't expired; each one is valid for 10 minutes.

**The network.** Switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data and make sure the signal is stable before requesting another code.

**The information is already in use.** If the system says your email or phone is registered, try logging in via password recovery. If you're certain you never created the account, contact support.

If the phone number field rejects your format, the usual culprits are a wrong country code, the country code typed twice, stray spaces, an accidental leading zero, or a number that doesn't match the local format. Once a country code is selected on the page, you generally don't repeat it in the number field. This is the single most common error in the whole flow.

Two recovery routes exist for the messier cases: OKX documents how to change your mobile number, and how to migrate an email address or phone number if you end up holding one unverified account plus one verified account under the same details. Sub-accounts can have a phone number linked, but never one already registered to another account.

## FAQ

**Can I have both an individual and a corporate account?**
Yes, one of each. Apply separately and verify each one through its own process. The individual account must be registered with your own personal information.

**Can I convert my individual account into a corporate one later?**
Generally no. They're different account types. If you need a corporate account, apply through the corporate registration portal rather than trying to upgrade.

**I'm 17. Can a parent consent and let me register?**
No. You must meet the minimum age shown on the registration page, and parental consent, financial circumstances or other personal reasons can't create an exception. Using a parent's or guardian's identity information to register breaks the one-document-one-account rule and puts the account at risk.

**Can I open two accounts with one email address?**
No. Each phone number and each email address can register only one OKX account, and both a phone number and an email address must be bound during registration. The page decides what it actually asks for, but the one-to-one rule holds.

**Does successful registration mean I can use every product?**
No. Available trading products, payment methods, deposit and withdrawal options and derivatives services depend on your service entity, country of residence, account type and completed checks. Registering and verifying gets you an account, not a guarantee of the full product set.
